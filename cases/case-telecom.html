Churn prediction for a telecom operator.

Designed and trained a churn prediction model on behavioral, usage, billing, and service-interaction signals — built not just to score churn risk accurately, but to surface the underlying reasons so that retention teams could intervene with something more specific than a discount offer.

§ Overview

Churn risk everyone could agree was real, and nobody could act on.

Telecom churn is famously well-studied and operationally frustrating. Models that predict churn risk well enough are not the hard part; the hard part is that a churn score by itself isn't actionable. The retention team's default lever — call the customer and offer a discount — costs margin and tends to be wrong as often as right when applied to the broad population the model flagged.

The engagement's framing was deliberate from the start:

  • Build a churn prediction model that worked well enough to be operationally useful (calibrated, with sensible precision at the recall levels the retention team could actually act on).
  • Engineer features that captured the kinds of churn that matter operationally — competitive switching, service-quality frustration, billing-shock churn, lifecycle churn — because the right intervention depends on which kind of risk you're looking at.
  • Make the model outputs interpretable, so retention agents could see not just "this customer is at risk" but "this customer is at risk and here's what's driving it."
  • Validate the system the way it would actually be used — by measuring the lift of retention interventions on flagged customers versus a control, not just AUC on a holdout set.
§ The Solution

Predict risk, and explain it in operational terms.

The system was built as a pipeline of feature engineering, model training, calibration, and explainability layered on customer data unified from billing, network usage, service interactions (call center contacts, ticket history), and account changes.

Feature engineering was where most of the model quality came from. Beyond standard usage and tenure features, I built behavioral-change features (declining usage trends, mode shifts in channel of contact, escalating ticket sentiment), billing-event features (recent bill shocks, plan changes, promotional roll-off), and service-quality proxies (dropped call rates, data session failures in the customer's typical usage cells).

Modeling used gradient-boosted trees as the primary churn classifier — calibrated for probability accuracy, not just rank-ordering, because the retention team needed actual probabilities to size interventions.

The interpretability layer surfaced per-customer the top feature contributions to that customer's churn score. This meant the model output to retention wasn't just "0.78 risk" — it was "0.78 risk, primary drivers: 30% decline in usage over six weeks, recent billing increase from promotional roll-off, two unresolved service tickets in the last 60 days." That changes the intervention completely.

Validation was operational, not just statistical. Beyond standard model metrics, the system was evaluated against retention campaign outcomes: when the retention team acted on model-flagged customers with reason-appropriate offers, did churn drop versus a holdout control? That's the metric that actually mattered.

§ Results

Targeted retention, not blanket discounts.

The model worked. More usefully, it shifted the retention team's posture from "blanket-call the model's top decile" to "act on the model's flags with interventions that fit the reason."

  • Calibrated churn probabilities retention leadership could use to size intervention budgets and prioritize agent capacity meaningfully.
  • Reason-coded risk per flagged customer meant retention conversations started with what was actually wrong — not a generic save offer.
  • Measurable lift over control on retention campaigns that used reason-appropriate interventions, validating the model in the way that mattered operationally.
  • Lower margin leakage on discount-only saves as the team moved away from defaulting to price interventions for every flagged customer.
  • Operational handoff with documentation, retraining cadence, and monitoring set up so the model could continue running after the engagement.
