Customer segmentation for RBC advisor operations.

A four-month Tepper capstone with the Royal Bank of Canada: behavior-based customer segmentation, a classification model to assign new clients to segments, and a prioritized advisor playbook that mapped each segment to outreach actions ranked by client account priority.

§ Overview

An advisory team flying with demographic-only segments.

RBC's advisory team was prioritizing client outreach using broad demographic buckets — age band, net worth tier, product holdings. These segments were stable and easy to maintain. They were also weakly correlated with what advisors actually needed to know: how a client was engaging, what life-stage signals were showing up in their behavior, and which accounts genuinely warranted advisor time this quarter.

The team needed sharper signals. The capstone scope:

  • Build behavior-based segments that captured engagement and lifecycle patterns, not just demographics.
  • Stand up a classification model that could assign new and existing clients to those segments at scale.
  • Translate segments into an advisor playbook — for each segment, the recommended outreach approach, key questions, and the prioritization logic that ranked accounts within the segment by attention warranted.
  • Deliver the work in a format non-technical decision-makers (advisors, regional leads) could read, trust, and act on.
§ The Solution

Features first, models second, playbook last.

The work was sequenced deliberately: a heavy feature-engineering phase, then segmentation, then the predictive layer, then the operational packaging — because the value of the model depends entirely on the features fed into it.

Feature engineering drew on banking transaction patterns, product holdings, channel engagement (branch, mobile, advisor contact), and lifecycle indicators. We engineered features capturing engagement intensity, channel mix, behavioral change over time, and product-relationship breadth — chosen because they corresponded to questions advisors actually ask about clients.

Segmentation used clustering on the engineered feature space, with deliberate work on segment count, stability across time windows, and interpretability. A segment that's mathematically clean but doesn't correspond to a recognizable client type is useless to an advisor; we optimized for both.

Classification models in Python assigned new and existing clients to segments. Holdout testing confirmed segment stability over time, and segment profiles were validated against business KPIs — average product holdings, revenue, retention — to confirm they corresponded to meaningful client realities.

The advisor playbook was the actual deliverable. For each segment we produced: a one-page description (who they are, what they want, what they're signaling), the recommended outreach cadence and channel, conversation starters tied to the segment's behavioral patterns, and an account-prioritization score that ranked clients within each segment by where advisor attention would have the highest expected lift.

Interactive dashboards let advisors see their book filtered and ranked by segment and priority score, with the option to drill into the reasoning behind any individual account's ranking.

§ Results

A playbook the advisory team actually wanted to use.

The capstone concluded with a working segmentation, a deployable classification model, and — most importantly — a playbook that advisors found credible enough to engage with rather than dismiss as a model output.

  • Behavior-based segments that advisors could recognize from their own client conversations, replacing demographic buckets that didn't correspond to how clients actually behaved.
  • A stable classification model with validated segment assignments that held up under holdout testing across time windows.
  • An account prioritization score per segment so the question shifted from "who in my book should I call this week" to "the system thinks these three accounts are the highest-leverage outreach this week, here's why."
  • Recommended playbooks per segment grounded in the behavioral signals that defined each segment, with conversation starters that gave advisors a concrete on-ramp.
  • Stakeholder-facing dashboards that translated model outputs into a format advisors could read and act on without analyst translation.
